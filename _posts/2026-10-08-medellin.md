Colombia has been such a social country for us in the sense of spending time in the company of people who become lifelong connections. From our first stop in the country, where <a href="/colombia/shipping/">we met Monica</a> - who went to Duke while we were at NCSU - to Cajicá, where we were <a href="/colombia/homeAndCajica/">taken in by the Sastres</a>, to now Medellín, where we reunited with Siobhan, a friend we made in a <a href="/nicaragua/nicaragua/">one-day stop in Granada, Nicaragua</a> who then also helped us <a href="/CostaRica/CRNI/">when we faced issues crossing into Costa Rica</a>. Siobhan is one of those people who is warm and kind and open to everyone. The day we met, C and I had decided to join a walking tour to maximize our 24 hours in Granada, where we met Siobhan and decided to go to lunch together afterwards. (Pro tip: one of the best ways to make friends while traveling is to join a walking tour and invite people out to lunch afterwards, something Siobhan does regularly!). What had started as a day exploring the colonial town became an afternoon filled with good snacks spent in the presence of a stranger who became a quick friend.

<figcaption>A theme of the weekend was walking tours; this was one from the city center in Medellín.</figcaption> 

Fast forward to now. As luck would have it, Siobhan had mentioned she would be in Medellín around the end of September; while we initially did not think our paths would cross, luck and a little bit of planning made our time in the city line up perfectly with her 70th birthday bash!! A bash full of wonderful women from all stages of Siobhan's life who had flown into Medellín for the weekend to celebrate this wonderful person. A weekend full of sisterhood, so much delicious food, and (of course) tons of walking tours. From walking around the botanical garden re-experiencing the joy of being surronded by tropical plants, savoring vegetarian <em>ceviche</em>, a hot-tub pool party, and, most importantly, endulging in Colombian candy with Natalie, who reignited my sweet tooth (it's a problem every time we go to the grocery store now), my heart was full from being included in this weekend and from the wonderful friendships and memories made. 

{% include gallery.html id="girlfriends" %}
<figcaption>Snapshots of our weekend together with Siobhan and friends!</figcaption> 


Being a nomad can get pretty lonely, but the connections we have made and strengthened in Colombia - where strangers have truly become a part of our life - have taken some of that loneliness away. Our time in this country has been different, to say the least, but the Colombian community has welcomed us in a way that is truly astounding. 

Besides sisterhood and community, there was also tons of food and sugar during our time here. As part of C and I's wedding anniversary, we went to a vegan Asian-Latin fusion restaurant, Ginger Cocina, for a six-course tasting that was very delicious, comforting, and a nice culmination of flavors from our travels so far. There was fermented mushroom paste with sautéed mushroom tacos, a tofu-pesto-tomato soup in vegetable broth, and a unique mango-chocolate-peanut butter dessert, to name a few of the courses. Over our few weeks in Medellín, we also had a delicious vegetarian <em>menu del dia</em> at Saludpan containing stuffed tofu eggplant and a very hearty Thai soup, some thick, juicy pancakes at Gardenia Brunch, a very Barbie marshmallow milkshake at Cafe 88, and some good pizza around town. To say we ate well would be an understatement.

Between all of the meals, we found nice street parking in some pretty cute neighborhoods with wonderful public libraries that we enjoyed working at, and local "watering holes" where the community would come out and enjoy the fresh air after afternoon rain showers. The way of life here is (relatively) slow and relaxed, right in line with our travels. People spend mornings at their local <em>panadería</em> with delicious pastries, a typical breakfast, and a <em>tinto</em> (coffee). Evenings are spent in the parks, walking around and just being outside. I could picture living here in Colombia... clearly we have enjoyed it so far, now going on our 5th month in the country. Even the rainy season has my heart, for with every downpour comes a nice coolness to the air that I cherish. Nearly as much as I cherish the friends and connections we've made here.
