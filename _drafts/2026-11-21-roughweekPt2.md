When you've been on the road for <a href="/colombia/stinkbugYear2/">more than a year</a>, you'll experience a setback or two. Statistically (and, perhaps even moreso, psychologically), sometimes they will occur all at once and you'll be forced to adapt on the fly. We've <a href="/ElSalvador/roughweek/">been through some things</a>, and have gotten much better at maintaining our peace and remaining flexible when sh*t happens, as it will inevitably will. I guess whichever God watches over overlanders decided it was time to put that hard-earned resilience to the test.

Ready for some new scenery, we had a long drive planned to leave <a href="/colombia/EjeCafetero/">the coffee region in the central Andes</a> and descend into the valley containing the Tatacoa Desert. We made it less than an hour before I lost control of steering and had to pull over to the side of the road. A Google search and quick check inside the engine confirmed we wouldn't be going anywhere anytime fast thanks to a completely shredded serpentine belt...

Where a helpful (and opportunistic) restaurantuer whose roadside place we parked in front of quickly came over to offer us refreshments and see if he could offer his aid. Enzo and his wife were incredibly kind to us, as he organized a local mechanic to come take a look at our car while he ran into town to get a replacement belt while she kept us fed, watered, and cool in the shade of their tables. Within a few hours, we were back on the road. Having missed our day to put some miles down, we returned to Armenia for one more night, disappointed at our setback but grateful that our car was still running and lucky to have one more morning to spend with our friend Carlos.

The next day, we were indeed able to leave Armenia and, after a stop at a small river to complete a load of laundry, we parked in the town of Ibague for the evening. With no spots recommended on the iOverlander app, we put our own discrete camping skills to the test and found a pleasant park on the outskirts of town, while a couple of other young people enjoyed the evening nearby. Only, as we started to get ready for bed, the number of other people kept growing until we found ourselves in the middle of a full blown party. A party that was only ended when the cops showed up. After talking to the officers, we were given permission to stay, but a warning that - despite the recent evacuation of the area - we could expect the noise and smell to return. Sure enough, as we tried to fall asleep, new arrivals to the park started playing their music and enjoying themselves: even worse, after we finally fell asleep, we were awoken by a knock at the door...

\addpics?{did we get any of the cops?}

And it was the cops again (although a different set this time). They were again friendly to us and sympathetic that we were awoken again but, to prevent further disruption, insisted we follow them to a quieter parking lot. And so, days after our first time breaking down on the road, we can now also claim to have driven for the first time with someone still in bed. 

The parking lot the police recommended was indeed much quieter and the rest of the night passed without incident. Tired but determined to keep going, we finally made it to Tatacoa the following day and were rewarded for our determination with <a href="/colombia/tatacoa/">some truly spectacular views</a>. Butttt, while we wanted nothing more than to enjoy the landscape and hide from the midday heat, we had another problem: our house batteries were nearly dead...

\st{Because, when we were attempting to repair our car by cleaning the battery terminals (a <a href="/colombia/shipping/">common problem of ours</a>), our electronic box that converts the power from our alternator to our house batteries seemed to reset.}

Unfortunately for us, we only noticed this problem because our batteries ran perilously low, a particularly pernicious situation in the desert where our fridge is working overtime. Google was our friend again, as we searched for some time to find a way to reset the converter in hopes we could charge again as we set off the next morning. In the meantime, we utilized the cool(er) morning to explore the arid steppes on our bikes and basked in the wonderful starlight of the open skies at night.
