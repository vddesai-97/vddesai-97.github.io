Having lived on the road for nearly a year now, the word "home" has a multitude of meanings for us. In the most immediate sense, our van is, of course, our home on wheels, where we go to sleep and to reset. Given that we are constantly on the move, we will often refer to the street or neighborhood where we parked as "home" as well, so that "heading home" could indicate simply returning to the next-door park or a regularly frequented cafe. Of course, home also means the U.S., although we may be referring to our family homes (Indiana and Minnesota) or our adopted state of North Carolina. And finally, occasionally, we will be lucky enough to be invited into someone else's home so that it becomes our temporary one for a short period, a wonderful gift to break up some of the difficulties **solitude** of life on the road.

In this way, during the month of August, we needed to leave our home (the Stinkbug) in our current home country (Colombia) to get some repairs completed while we returned home (the US, to all three states and then some) to visit family. While balancing the logistics of all these moving parts, we met one of the kindest families on our journey to date, who generously welcomed us into their home and gave us a base from which we could organize the various errands and hurdles as we left and returned to van life on either side of our travels.

Cajica, a town of extreme kindness. A suburb north of Bogota -- where we would be flying from in a week -- we attempted to make it our final stop before entering the city; in part because Bogota restricts days we are able to drive (called <em>pico y placa</em>: an effort to reduce congestion and emissions) and in part because we agreed to meet up with friends there (our container buddies!) for a final meal of Indian food before they continued on to Ecuador and beyond. However, it turned out that parking anywhere in town was complicated, with small and crowded streets not suitable for overnight parking and no suggestions on the iOverlander app. We tried our luck at the plaza of Cajica, where a farmer's market was closing for the day. After inquiring with the security guard, however, we learned that extended parking was not allowed here either. Fortuitously, as we had been pulling in, several guys had been really excited upon seeing our car and the bikes on it. It turned out that they worked at the market, and were nearby as we made our request. As we trudged back to the van, resigned to drive around more in search of parking, one of them hurried over and -- within a minute of meeting us and hearing about our parking quest -- offered a spot outside his home. This is how we came to know Fabian and the Sastres.

Our time at home **(in the U.S.) was not static. Constantly on the move in some form or fashion. We visited 8 states and 9 homes, and traveled via both cars and airplanes. There was also a shift between the first half of our trip and the second half. A shift from wanting to be back on the road to being nervous about going back to the Stinkbug. This shift led to some reflections within ourselves and together of how we wanted the next couple of years to look like for us. While we plan to finish our time in South America slowly and luxuriously, we are also looking forward to coming back, settling down and having a community within reach.
