Colombia has been such a social country for usXXX, andXXX not in the sense of partying or vacation friends that you never talk to again after the trip is over, but in the sense of spending time in the company of people who are lifelong. From our first stop in the country where we met Monica - who went to Duke while we were at NCSU - to Cajica, where we were taken in by the Sastres, to now Medellin, where we met back up with Siobhan, a friend we made in a one-day stop in Nicaragua who then also helped us when we faced issues crossing into Costa Rica. Siobhan is one of those people who is warm and kind and opening. THhe day we met, C and I had decided to join a walking tour to maximize our 24 hours in Granada. While on a walking tour, we met Siobhan and decided to go to lumch together afterwards. Pro tip: one of the best ways to make friends is to join a walking tour and invite people out to lunch afterwards. What had started out as a day exploring Granada became an afternoon eating good food and snacks with a stranger that became a friend. Fast forward to now and as luck would have it, Siobhan had mentioned she would be in Medellin around the end of September, and while we initially did not think our paths would cross, luck and a little bit of planning would make our time in the city line up perfectly for her 70th birthday bash!! A bash fulled of wonderful women from all stages of Siobhan's life that had flown into Medellin for the weekend to celebrate this wonderful person.

A weekend full of sisterhood, such delicious food, and tons of walking tours. My heart was full from being included in this weekend and from the wonderful friendships and memories made. 

Being a nomad can get pretty lonely and the connections we have made in Colombia where strangers have become a part of our life have taken some of that loneliness away. Our time here in this country has been different, to say the least, but the Colombian community that has welcomed us in is truly astounding. 

Besides sisterhood and community, there was also tons of food and sugar during our time here. As part of our wedding anniversary, we went to a vegan asian-latin fusion restuarent, Ginger Cocina, for a 6 course tasting that was very delicious, comforting, and a nice culmination of flavors of our travels so far. There was fermeneted mushroom paste with sauteed mushroom tacos and a tofu-pesto-tomato-soup in vegetable broth, and unqiue mango-chocolate-peanut butter dessert to name a few of the courses. Additionally over our few weeks in Medellin, we had a delicious vegetarian <em>menu del dia</em> at Saludpan containing stuffed tofu eggplant and a very hearty thai soup, some thick juicy pancakes at Gardenia Brunch, a very barbie marshmellow milkshake at Cafe 88, and some good pizza around town. To say we ate well is an understatement. 

\addpics{food!!!}

Between all the meals, we also were granted some nice street parking in some pretty cute neighborhoods that had wonderful public libraries that we enjoyed working at and local "watering holes" where the communities would come out and enjoy the cool air after the afternoon rain showers. Over these past several months, the way of life here is slow and relaxed. People spend mornings at their local panadera with delicous pastries and typical breakfast with a side of tinto. Evenings are spent in the parks, walking around, and just being outside. I could see a life living here in Colombia...clearly we have enjoyed it so far, now going on our 5th month in the country. Even the rainy season has my heart, for with every downpour there is nice coolness to the air that I cherish.
