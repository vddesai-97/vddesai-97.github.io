Colombia has been such a social country for us, and not in the sense of partying or vacation friends that you never talk to again after the trip is over, but in the sense of spending time in the company of people who are lifelong. From our first stop in the country, where we met Monica who went to Duke while we were at NCSU, to Cajica, where we were taken in by the Sastres, to now Medellin, where we met back up with Siobhan, a friend we made in our one day stop in Granada, Nicaragua, who then helped us when we faced issues crossing into Costa Rica. Siobhan is one of those people who is warm and kind and opening and while on a walking tour, we both wanted to invite the other to lunch afterwards, and what was meant to be a day exploring Granada, became instead a day of hanging out with a stranger that instantly became a good friend. As luck would have it, she had mentioned she would be in Medellin around the end of September, and while initially we did not think our paths would cross as we might miss her by a month, luck and a little bit of planning would make our time out to medellin line up perfectly for her 70th birthday bash!! A bash fulled of wonderful women from all stages of Siobhan's life that had flown into Medellin for the weekend to celebrate this wonderful person. 

A weekend full of sisterhood, such delicious food, and tons of walking tours. My heart was full from being included into this weekend of lifelong friends of Siobhan's that I believe I also made some wonderful friendships along the way. One thing that Siobhan said over this weekend that struck a chord with me is the loneliness of being a nomad and how this weekend, and Clark's and mine's overall time in Colombia, had been filled with such wonderful people that have made lasting impact on us. Our time here in this country has been different, to say the least, but the Colombian community that has welcomed us in is truly astounding. 

Besides sisterhood and community, there was also tons of food and sugar during our time here. As part of our wedding anniversary, we went to a vegan asian-latin fusion restuarent, Ginger Cocina, for a 6 course tasting. Sounds expensive, but in reality it was very delicious, comforting, and a nice culmination of flavors of our travels so far. There was fermeneted mushroom paste with sauteed mushroom tacos and a tofu-pesto-tomato-soup in vegetable broth, and unqiue mango-chocolate dessert to name a few of the courses. Additionally over our few weeks in Medellin, we had a delicious vegetarian menu del dia at Saludpan conatining stuffed tofu eggplant and a very hearty thai soup, some thick juicy pancakes at Gardenia, a very barbie marshmellow milkshake at Cafe 88, and some good pizza around town. To say we ate well is an understatement.
